# US Market Legal Readiness Checklist

**Status:** Operational checklist — NOT legal or fiscal advice.  
**Date:** 2026-06-14  
**Prepared by:** Luma Premium

> **IMPORTANT:** This document is an operational checklist and does not substitute qualified legal or tax advice. Before conducting business in the United States, retain a licensed attorney and/or CPA with experience in international business and digital services.

---

## 1. Business structure

- [ ] Determine appropriate US entity type (LLC, C-Corp, S-Corp)
- [ ] Evaluate Delaware or Wyoming LLC for non-resident founders (common choice)
- [ ] Obtain EIN (Employer Identification Number) from IRS
- [ ] Open US business bank account (Relay, Mercury, or similar fintech for non-residents)
- [ ] Consider Registered Agent requirement for the state of incorporation
- [ ] Review treaty benefits between Dominican Republic and US (no full tax treaty — verify with advisor)

## 2. International contracts

- [ ] Draft service agreement template in English with:
  - Scope of work
  - Deliverables and timeline
  - Payment terms and currency (USD)
  - Intellectual property assignment
  - Limitation of liability
  - Governing law and jurisdiction clause
  - Dispute resolution (arbitration vs. litigation)
- [ ] Include a confidentiality / NDA section
- [ ] Localize for state-specific requirements if targeting specific states (CA, NY, TX, FL differ significantly)

## 3. Invoicing & payment

- [ ] Issue invoices in USD with proper business entity name and EIN
- [ ] Accept international wire (SWIFT/ACH), credit cards, Stripe, or PayPal Business
- [ ] Consider Wise Business for receiving USD
- [ ] Document all transactions for tax filing purposes
- [ ] Understand potential 1099 / W-9 / W-8BEN requirements when US clients pay non-US entities

## 4. Tax forms US clients may request

| Form | When required |
|------|--------------|
| W-8BEN-E | Non-US entity receiving US-source income — certifies foreign status |
| W-9 | Only if you have US tax identity (SSN or EIN) |
| 1099-NEC | Issued BY your US client to you if payments exceed $600/year |

- [ ] Prepare W-8BEN-E in advance — US enterprise clients will request it before payment
- [ ] Consult CPA on whether services qualify as US-source income under Chapter 3 withholding rules

## 5. Privacy policy & data protection

- [ ] Publish Privacy Policy in English on `lumapremium.com`
- [ ] Include:
  - What data is collected and why
  - How it is stored (Google Sheets/Drive — disclose)
  - Third-party tools used
  - User rights (access, deletion, correction)
  - Contact method for privacy requests
- [ ] California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA): if serving CA residents, add CCPA rights notice
- [ ] Do NOT represent GDPR compliance unless EU residents are actively targeted
- [ ] Cookie consent banner if using analytics (GA4, Meta Pixel, etc.)

## 6. Terms of service

- [ ] Publish Terms of Service in English
- [ ] Include:
  - Acceptance clause
  - Service description and limitations
  - Payment and refund policy
  - Intellectual property
  - Disclaimers and limitation of liability
  - Governing law
- [ ] Separate Terms for different service tiers if pricing differs significantly

## 7. Commercial communications compliance

### Email outreach (CAN-SPAM Act)
- [ ] All commercial emails must include:
  - Physical mailing address (US or international)
  - Unsubscribe mechanism
  - Honest subject lines
  - Sender identification
- [ ] Honor opt-out requests within 10 business days
- [ ] Do not use deceptive routing information

### Phone / WhatsApp outreach (TCPA)
- [ ] Obtain prior express written consent before calling or texting US mobile numbers for commercial purposes
- [ ] TCPA violations: $500–$1,500 per message/call — this is a high-risk area
- [ ] Do NOT send unsolicited commercial WhatsApp messages to US numbers without consent
- [ ] Consider a consent checkbox on the assessment form specifically for US prospects

### Cold outreach / LinkedIn
- [ ] LinkedIn messaging is generally acceptable for B2B prospecting
- [ ] Do not make misleading claims about identity or affiliation
- [ ] No automated mass messaging (violates LinkedIn ToS and may trigger TCPA)

## 8. State-specific considerations

| State | Key note |
|-------|----------|
| California | CCPA applies; most aggressive consumer protection laws |
| New York | Strong consumer protection; local counsel advised for enterprise deals |
| Texas | Business-friendly; moderate compliance burden |
| Florida | Large Hispanic market; moderate compliance |
| Puerto Rico | US territory; CAN-SPAM and TCPA apply; no state income tax on certain income |

- [ ] Review regulations for each target state before active campaigns
- [ ] Retain state-licensed attorney for contracts executed in specific states

## 9. Content compliance

- [ ] Remove any unverified revenue claims ("clients made $X", "we sold $X in properties")
- [ ] Do not display third-party logos without written authorization
- [ ] Mark demos clearly as "Demo oficial" or "Official demo" — not production client sites
- [ ] Testimonials must be genuine and not fabricated
- [ ] Income or results claims require substantiation (FTC Endorsement Guides)
